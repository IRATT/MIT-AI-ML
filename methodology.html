Evidence & authorship

A portfolio with a clear evidence trail.

A distinction between course achievement, submitted analysis and ideas for future application.

Reviewed · 4 November 2026

Source of the record

The course catalogue, all 24 module overviews, the authenticated final gradebook and available submission pages were reviewed. The gradebook records 144 graded activities totaling 813 out of 813 points. Scores are final recorded results, including accepted resubmissions.

What is included

  • 24 original module summaries with topic, work, learning insight, potential application and final activity scores.
  • Analysis informed by 19 recovered submitted files and five submitted text responses, retained privately.
  • Four public, reviewed numerical CSV extracts and one screened Gurobipy notebook with static outputs.
  • Four case-study syntheses connecting the coursework to decisions and implementation.
  • Five program takeaways and a reflection on data, decisions and economic value, adapted from the closing slide and 1:17 wrap-up supplied by Ian, with links to the reviewed coursework.
  • The official completion-certificate image and two exemplary-assignment badge images, with public verification links.

Following the portfolio guidance

The course’s “Developing Your Portfolio” guidance recommends using GitHub to showcase completed work, including Google Colab notebooks and noncoding interpretations. It states that Codio assignments are not available for transfer. Accordingly, those modules include clear summaries and final grades without presenting reconstructed code as an original submission.

Authorship and assistance

This is Ian Alleyne’s learner portfolio. The educational cases, starter code and templates originated with the course and their respective authors. Submitted responses and interpretations are attributed to Ian; use of a course-provided notebook does not imply original authorship of the underlying case or starter implementation.

AI assistance was used to organize, edit and build this portfolio. Some original submissions also disclose AI assistance with organizing thoughts. The public summaries preserve the substance of the work while adding explicit limitations where necessary. Full original papers and their embedded images are not published.

Results and claims

Numerical results are identified as course-case results or recorded model outputs. Proposed operational applications and transformation benefits are not presented as measured deployments. Final course grades should not be read as an independent audit of every technical detail.

Quality checks identified a C3/C4 transcription discrepancy between the Module 13 written dashboard and executed notebook. The case study follows the notebook and documents the discrepancy. An independent linear-program check reproduces the network objective and the Module 9.2 product mix; original papers and workbooks remain private. The public notebook has interface metadata removed, while the CSV files are explicitly labeled as reviewed extracts.

Public boundaries

This site excludes private gradebook screenshots, learner identifiers, session links, other participants’ submissions and lecture transcripts. It contains no patient records or private repository contents. The alarm-management strategy is an independent portfolio synthesis; it does not state a company-approved operating plan or employment appointment.

Maintenance

The underlying source is a small, static website. Module data is stored separately from the page generator, so evidence, corrections and new work can be added consistently. See the portfolio plan for the content structure and update workflow.
